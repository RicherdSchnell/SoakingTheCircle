Best for a treat

Laugarás Lagoon

Just off Route 30, above the Hvítá river valley. About 1.5 hours from Reykjavík.

  • Upper, lower and forest pools, plus a waterfall feature and a grotto of basalt columns
  • Two swim-up bars and dry saunas looking over the river
  • Ylja restaurant on site, so you can stay for dinner

Best for atmosphere

Secret Lagoon

In the village of Flúðir, a short drive from Laugarás.

  • A natural pool in use since 1891
  • Steaming hot springs and a small geyser right beside the pool
  • Fewer frills than the newer spas, which is the appeal

Laugarvatn Fontana

On the shore of Lake Laugarvatn, between Þingvellir and Geysir.

  • Steam rooms sitting directly over a natural hot spring
  • Pools, plus a walk into the cold lake for contrast
  • Fits neatly at lunchtime, halfway round the circle

Before you go

Lagoon etiquette in Iceland

Every geothermal pool in Iceland expects you to shower without a swimsuit before you get in. It's a hygiene rule, not a suggestion, and staff will remind you.

  • Bring a swimsuit and a towel, or check if towels are included in your ticket.
  • Keep your hair out of the water or use conditioner, as minerals can leave it stiff.
  • Drink water. Long soaks in hot water dehydrate you faster than you'd think.
  • In winter, book a slot after dark: you might see the northern lights from the water.
